Key Milestones

Decisions That Changed the App

Building in public is only meaningful when community feedback actively changes the code. Here is how public scrutiny reshaped PoquitoTalk:

1
Aug 26–29

Another Builder on X Fixes My Paywall

I posted my raw paywall on X and got a blunt critique from Jay (@SlowbrewedS), an iOS product engineer. The same evening, the in-app paywall button got a color used nowhere else in the app (indigo #4F46E5). His follow-up swapped "Or continue with Free Version" for a simple "Try it first".

See the thread with Jay on X →
2
Sep 17

Incoming Voices Should Match the Real Speaker

A female boat operator's reply shouldn't come back in a male English voice. Your own voice setting only applies to the Spanish you send; incoming replies need to match the person who spoke. The fix is built so the contractor never has to do anything: the voice is guessed from the contact's name, the expat can change it with one tap, and unclear names like Guadalupe or "Taxi 25" get a neutral voice instead of a wrong one. It ships in v1.5.11, after v1.5.10 is live.

3
Sep 22

The Zero-Burden Principle (Peace Prize Submission)

After 14 years living in Bocas del Toro, I realized software built for social good cannot force behavioral changes onto local workers. If an app requires a boat captain steering through waves to download an English app, it fails. We placed 100% of the friction on the expat: outbound audio formats into native WhatsApp voice notes, paired with the zero-install 2-way web walkie link.

Read the full story on HackerNoon →
4
Sep 27

A Google Play Rejection, Treated as a Free Audit

Woke up to a rejection for v1.5.5 on a Sunday morning. It worked like a free audit: the reviewer caught a cold launch crash, a "$3.33/mo" price shown bigger than the $39.99 actually billed per year, and a mascot that vanished in release builds. We published the full post-mortem: diagnosing an animated WebP memory bug, making the yearly price the main price on the paywall, fixing Android 13 notification handlers, and shipping v1.5.9.

Read the Google Play Autopsy & AI Tennis Dispatch →
5
Sep 27

First Sale on the Web Funnel

A tourist visiting Bocas for 5 days will never commit to a $39.99/year app store subscription, so we added a one-time 50-credit pack on a web funnel: Stripe Checkout, synced into RevenueCat Web Billing, redeemable in the app. The first sale came from my mom in Belgium: $3.74 (€3.42). Every founder's first customer is family. Thanks, Mom.

Honest Log

What Didn't Work

Not every experiment paid off. These are from the public posts too.

Aug 20

YouTube Shorts went nowhere

Posted the first PoquitoTalk video to YT Shorts: close to 0 views.

A new channel starts at zero, however good the video is.

Aug 23

Too many ideas for one MVP

An OCR scanner for seafood menus, tourist vs. expat personas: all cut.

Not every good idea belongs in version 1.

Aug 25

Blocked from TikTok in Panama

Couldn't even create a second TikTok account over Starlink: security checks and carrier errors.

It took a clean, isolated macOS setup to finally get a working US account.

Sep 8

Waiting on Google Play review

Wanted to print T-shirts and start hitting the pavement in town, but had to wait for approval first.

Offline distribution depends on the store timeline. Plan around it.

Sep 19–22

The island tech tax

The solar inverter failed, the Starlink router died, and a flaky hotspot posted the same ship log three times.

Kept shipping on cellular data, one short log a day.

Sep 27

Rejected by Google Play

Version 1.5.5 was rejected for a crash, the pricing layout, and a missing mascot.

Fixed all three and shipped v1.5.9 the same day.
