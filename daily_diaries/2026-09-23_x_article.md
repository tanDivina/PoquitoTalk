### Building for Social Good: The Story Behind Our #shipathon Peace Prize Entry

I submitted my second HackerNoon article today: "14 Years in Bocas: Why I Built an App to Help Neighbors Talk."

In the RevenueCat Shipathon (#shipathon), there is a dedicated Peace Prize category for software built for social good and positive community impact. We will be entering PoquitoTalk for that award, and this new article explains the reasoning and philosophy behind that entry.

Living on this archipelago for fourteen years taught me how technology often separates people instead of bringing them together. Expats move here wanting to hire local boat captains, carpenters, and tour guides. But local tradespeople run their businesses entirely on Spanish voice notes sent through WhatsApp, recorded on the water over two-stroke outboard engine noise. Expats get nervous about their textbook Spanish, freeze up, and retreat into English-speaking bubbles.

When I built PoquitoTalk, I followed what I call the Zero-Burden Principle. Software built for community social good cannot force behavioral changes onto local workers. If an app requires a boat captain on open water to install software, create an account, or read translated text on a phone screen under direct sunlight, that app fails. The software has to keep all the friction on the user. PoquitoTalk formats Spanish audio directly into WhatsApp, uses respectful Panamanian greetings, and provides a web walkie at `/talk` so the recipient only has to tap play on a voice note.

### Fixing Organic Search for a 100% Cacao Calculator

On our cacao farm website, Finca Montezuma, we produce 100% pure unsweetened chocolate. Most baking recipes call for standard 70% dark chocolate or cocoa powder. When home bakers try to swap in pure cacao, the math gets complicated. You have to add sugar to balance the bitterness, and you have to cut added butter or oil because pure cacao mass contains roughly 50% cocoa butter.

We built an interactive recipe converter to calculate the exact ingredient swaps. This morning, Google Search Console showed the tool had zero impressions because it was unindexed. A useful calculator cannot bring in visitors if search crawlers cannot find it.

Today I fixed the crawl path by adding links to the tool in the global footer across the site. I also cleaned up the page itself, moving embedded JavaScript into `js/products-recipe-converter.js` and styles into `css/products-recipe-converter.css` to lower document weight and improve DOM parse speed. I added polite live regions for screen readers, verified canonical tags, and published clean markdown versions for AI search crawlers.

### Sourcing Builders from GitHub Instead of Twitter

On Velocity Radar, our cohort reached 1,439 builders. Manually searching Twitter for rising accounts with under 5,000 followers takes hours, and Twitter search results are packed with follow-for-follow pods, gain trains, and automated scripts.

Today I wrote an automated cloud discovery routine that sources candidates from GitHub's public developer search API. The script searches for profiles tagged with indie hacker, micro-saas, and build in public that link their X handles. When someone has public repositories and active commit history, you know they write code rather than gaming engagement algorithms.

Before adding accounts to the database, our filter checks three rules:
1. Following ratios. It drops accounts following over 2,000 people with a ratio above 1.2.
2. Bio text. It removes accounts containing phrases like follow back or gain train.
3. Post volume. It flags accounts with over 35,000 posts and fewer than 4,000 followers.

I also updated the Chrome extension background script to remove popup tabs so the extension runs in memory without disturbing client VPN sessions.

### The Sub-100 Micro-Breakout Metric

Most social analytics platforms track accounts with hundreds of thousands of followers. That data shows how large accounts retain attention, but it rarely shows how small accounts get noticed from scratch.

We added a scanner to track accounts under 100 followers that generate outsized views. We divide post views by the creator's follower count. Normal accounts under 100 followers see views equal to 5% or 10% of their follower count. Our scanner flags posts that reach four to fifteen times their follower count.

When an account with 60 or 80 followers generates hundreds of impressions, audience momentum played no role. The views came from hook structure, topic relevance, or participating in active community conversations under prominent builder accounts. Tracking those multiplier anomalies shows what creates early distribution before an audience exists.
